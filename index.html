Settings

Static

Radio-between-stations hiss. The quietest moments are used as a sample of it, then it is subtracted everywhere.

How hard the sampled hiss is subtracted.

The most any single frequency is turned down.

Kick and bass under this are barely touched.

Raise it if hiss remains; lower it if soft instruments go thin.

Shimmer

The glassy grain on cymbals and in the air above the mix. Brief, narrow flecks are pulled down; held notes and drum hits are kept.

Zero turns this stage off.

The most a single fleck is turned down.

Lower it if the grain reaches into vocals and guitars.

      How it works
      
The song is split into thousands of overlapping slices, about 43 milliseconds each, and each slice into a thousand frequency bands. Two passes then adjust the level of each band in each slice, and the slices are stitched back together with their original timing, so nothing about the attack or the stereo image moves.

      
The static pass follows the adaptive spectral denoise method shared on r/SunoAI: learn what the hiss sounds like from the quietest moments, subtract it gently in the bass and more firmly up top. The shimmer pass looks for sound that is both brief and narrow, the signature of that glassy grain, and pulls it down to the level of its surroundings. A drum hit rises across every band at once and a held note lasts, so both are left as they are.

      
MP3 input works, but it has already discarded detail and added its own artifacts, so start from WAV when you can. Download Cleaned or Removed as lossless WAV or a smaller MP3. MP3 supports mono and stereo; its sample rate may change to a supported rate and its encoder can add a short delay or padding. Keep WAV for further editing or exact alignment.

      
MP3 encoding uses lamejs and LAME, loaded locally with this page. Encoder source and licences.
